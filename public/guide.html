Money Guide: Paying and Getting Pesos in Argentina

Last reviewed: October 2026

The short version

  • Pay in pesos (cash) when you can. Pesos from Western Union or exchanging dollars get a better rate than any card, and many places give a 10-20% cash discount on top.
  • Use AMEX over Visa or Mastercard for the best card rate (on a card with no foreign transaction fee). In Buenos Aires, AMEX is accepted almost everywhere Visa is.
  • Always pay card charges in pesos, never in US dollars.
  • Get pesos with Western Union or another transfer service, or by exchanging US dollar bills.
  • Avoid ATMs and the street changers on Florida Street unless you have no other option.

Why cash is usually better

  • Better rate: pesos from Western Union or from exchanging dollars come in near or above the Blue rate. Card rates are lower (see the table below).
  • Cash discounts: many businesses take 10-20% off for cash ("efectivo"). Ask "¿Hay descuento en efectivo?"
  • Tips (about 10% at restaurants) are usually in cash.
  • Taxis, small shops and markets often don't take cards.

Paying by card

When you do use a card, the network converts your peso charge at its own daily rate. They differ a lot, and AMEX comes out ahead:

  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee. Otherwise your bank adds its fee (often around 3%) on top of the network's rate.
  • Always pay in pesos (ARS). If a card machine or ATM offers to charge you in US dollars, say no. That conversion uses the merchant's bank's rate, which is usually much worse.
  • Use AMEX first. In Buenos Aires it's accepted almost everywhere Visa is. Keep a Visa or Mastercard as a backup for the few places that don't take it.
Is Visa the MEP rate? Not quite. Visa's rate follows the previous day's MEP closely, which is why many articles say they're tied, but it has run about 6.5% below MEP throughout 2026.

Getting pesos

1. Western Union

Send money from your own US bank account to yourself, then pick up pesos at a WU location with your passport. You don't need to carry dollars or deal with street changers.

WU's rate is usually above Blue. For senders in the US, the fee brings the overall cost back close to Blue. Senders from most other countries pay much lower fees, which makes WU an even better deal.

See How to Get Pesos with WU.

2. Other transfer services (TapTap Send, Remitly, Wise)

Their rates are on the rates page, before fees. Check how each one pays out in Argentina before you sign up: some only pay into an Argentine bank account or wallet, which most visitors don't have.

3. Exchanging US dollar bills

  • Use an exchange house your hotel or host recommends. Expect a rate near Blue buy.
  • Bring clean, undamaged US$100 bills. Small, old or marked bills often get a worse rate or are refused.
  • Some small Western Union branches also buy dollars. Ask at the counter.

Last resorts

  • Florida Street changers: risk of fake bills and poor rates, and they often ask you to follow them to a second location.
  • ATMs: low limits per withdrawal and high fees per withdrawal.
