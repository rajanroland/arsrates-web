How We Get These Rates

Last reviewed: October 2026

Every rate on ARS Rates is in pesos per 1 US dollar. Market and money-transfer rates are checked every 15 minutes from 7:00 to 21:00 Argentina time, and hourly overnight. Card networks (Visa, Mastercard and AMEX) each set one rate per day for all charges that day, so those change once a day rather than during the day.

Sources

Rate Source Notes
Dólar Blue DolarHoy The informal market rate for cash dollars, as quoted by street exchange houses (cuevas).
Dólar Oficial, MEP, CCL DolarHoy Oficial is the banks' retail rate. MEP and CCL aren't set by a bank: they're market rates worked out from bond prices (bought in pesos, sold in dollars locally for MEP or abroad for CCL).
USDC DolarHoy The USDC dollar stablecoin on Argentine crypto exchanges.
Dólar Tarjeta Calculated Oficial sell × 1.30.
Dólar Mayorista BCRA (Central Bank) The wholesale reference rate (Comunicación A 3500), from the Central Bank's public data. Published on business days around 4 PM; we show the latest one.
Western Union Western Union WU's rate for sending dollars to pesos, before fees.
Wise, Remitly, TapTap Send Each company's public rate calculator Headline rates before fees. Remitly is its standard rate, not the first-transfer promo. TapTap Send is its US to Argentina rate.
Visa Visa One rate per day. Sunday and Monday use Saturday's rate.
Mastercard Mastercard One rate per day, published around 3 PM US Eastern time. Estimated until then (see below).
AMEX Our estimate from the BCRA rate See below. Replaced by the actual rate when we have a real charge for that day.

When DolarHoy is unavailable, the market rates come from dolarapi.com instead (it doesn't include USDC).

Estimates

A rate marked (Est.) is our estimate, shown until the real one is available.

  • Mastercard matches Visa on weekdays, so until Mastercard publishes its daily rate we show Visa's. On Saturday and Sunday, Mastercard keeps Friday's rate.
  • American Express converts peso charges at the Central Bank's A3500 rate from two business days before the charge, with no markup. We checked this against about 200 real charges from October 2025 to October 2026: the median difference was 0.04%. The charge shows its final dollar amount right away; AMEX used to charge the official rate and refund the difference later, but no longer does.

Reading the table

  • Buy / sell: buy is what you get when you sell a dollar; sell is what you pay to buy one.
  • VS 24H: the change from about 24 hours earlier.
  • VS BLUE: each rate compared with Blue buy. Green means more pesos per dollar than Blue.
  • Blank or "as of" time: every number is checked when it arrives (sensible range, correct currencies, buy not above sell). If a source fails, we show a blank, or the time of its last good value, rather than an old number that looks current.
