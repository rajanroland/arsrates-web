What Should I Use?

Last reviewed: November 2026

There are four common ways to pay in Argentina without a local bank account. The easier ones cost more. Type what you expect to spend and see what each one costs at today's rates, so you can decide whether the savings are worth the effort.

1. Card for most things, ATM for small cash

With Visa or Mastercard. With AMEX: –.

What it takes: nothing beyond the card you have, plus an ATM withdrawal of about 100,000 pesos for tips and small purchases.

Why it costs more: card rates give fewer pesos per dollar than cash, and paying by card misses the 10-15% cash discounts many places give. Not counted above: ATM fees, and any foreign transaction fee on your card (how to check).

2. Pesos from Western Union

–

Sending from a WU location at home (fee about 1.6%). Sending online by debit card (about 7%): –.

What it takes: a WU account from home, then a trip to a WU location in Argentina with your passport, sometimes with a line. See the WU guide and the WU finder.

Large amounts late in the month: locations run short of pesos toward the end of the month, even WU's own branches. Pick up large amounts earlier in the month if you can, go when they open, and have a second location in mind.

Some effort Cheapest now

3. Pesos from US dollar bills

–

What it takes: bringing US$100 bills in perfect condition (current design, clean, no marks) and changing them at a licensed exchange house. Older, marked or smaller bills get a lower rate, and you carry the cash with you.

Most setup Cheapest now

4. Wander Wallet for QR payments, plus ATM or WU for cash

–

Includes its fees: $2 + 0.3% to load the wallet from a US bank account, and $0.20 per payment (counted as one payment per US$20).

What it takes: verifying your US bank account, which can take several days to a week, so set it up before you travel. Many places take QR payments, but you'll still want some cash for tips and places that don't.

Check the fee in the app before you send. Then pay by bank transfer or QR, or take cash out of your account.

Most other options need an Argentine bank account (TapTap, Remitly, Wise, ARQ) or a crypto exchange or contact in Argentina that you trust. The rates page has all of them.

Does my card charge a foreign transaction fee?

The costs above assume it doesn't. To check:

  1. Pay for something in Argentina by card and note the amount in pesos. Your statement may not show it.
  2. When the charge posts, divide the pesos by the dollars you were charged.
  3. Compare that with the Visa, Mastercard or AMEX rate for the day it was processed, usually the day of the purchase or 1-2 days later. Today's is on the rates page, earlier days on the charts.

About the same: no fee. About 3% fewer pesos: a foreign transaction fee. About 1% fewer: likely a markup by your card issuer.
