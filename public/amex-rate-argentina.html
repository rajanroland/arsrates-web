What Exchange Rate Does AMEX Use in Argentina?

Last reviewed: November 2026

Short answer: AMEX converts peso charges at the Argentine Central Bank's wholesale rate (A3500) from two business days before the charge, with no markup. On a US card, that has usually meant more pesos per dollar than Visa or Mastercard.

How we checked

AMEX doesn't publish this rate for Argentina. So we compared real charges on a US-issued AMEX card with the Central Bank's published A3500 rate from two business days earlier. The sample covers 207 days of charges, from August 2025 to October 2026.

Typical (median) difference0.04%
Charges within 0.1%68%
Charges within 0.5%87%

Most of the misses are small. Some charges match the previous day's A3500 instead, likely when the merchant submits the charge the same day. So treat the rule as a very good estimate, not a guarantee for every charge.

How it compares with Visa and Mastercard

Since August 2025, this A3500 rate has typically been 4.7% above Visa's rate for the same day. The gap moves: on 90% of days it was between 1.3% and 6.7%. Mastercard matches Visa on weekdays.

On a 100,000-peso bill, a 4.7% gap is roughly the difference between paying about $66 and about $69, as in our dinner example.

Does AMEX still refund the difference later?

AMEX used to charge the official rate, then refund the difference later. On our charges now, the charge shows its final dollar amount right away.

What to do with this

  • If you have an AMEX, it's usually the better card to use. Today's estimate is on the rates page.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee. That fee is often another 3%.
  • We've tested acceptance across Buenos Aires (CABA), not elsewhere yet. Keep a Visa or Mastercard as a backup. More in the Money Guide.
  • Paying in cash can still beat any card, especially with a cash discount.
