Is the Visa Rate the MEP Rate in Argentina?

Last reviewed: November 2026

Short answer: No. Visa's rate follows the previous day's MEP rate, but it has run about 6.5% below it. That's a pattern in our data, not a formula Visa publishes.

What we measured

We compared Visa's published rate each day with the previous day's MEP sell rate, from November 2025 to early November 2026 (263 days). We left out Sundays and Mondays, because Visa reuses Saturday's rate on those days.

Typical (median) Visa rate, as a share of the prior day's MEP93.5%
Range on 90% of days92.7% to 94.1%
Monthly medians, November 2025 to October 202693.4% to 93.7%

The discount has been very steady. So if the previous day's MEP sell rate was 1,600, you could expect Visa near 1,496. Treat that as a rough guide, and check the actual rate on the rates page.

Why people think they're the same

Visa's rate does move with the previous day's MEP more closely than with any other rate we track. So it's easy to describe them as tied. But tied isn't equal. A card charge at Visa's rate gets you about 6.5% fewer pesos per dollar than the MEP rate from the day before.

What about Mastercard and AMEX?

  • Mastercard matched Visa on weekdays over the same period (median ratio 1.0000).
  • AMEX works differently: it uses the Central Bank's wholesale rate, which has usually meant more pesos. See what rate AMEX uses.
