How to Get Pesos with Western Union

Last reviewed: October 2026

How it works

You send money from your own US bank account or debit card to yourself, then pick up the pesos in cash at a WU location in Argentina with your passport.

The exchange rate is set when you send. The WU rate on our rates page is WU's rate before fees, so check the rate and fee in the app before you send.

Why WU: exchange houses pay the best rate only for US$100 bills in perfect condition, and most people have a limited supply. So use WU whenever its rate after fees is close to Blue. Rates swing, though: at times Blue is above WU even before fees.

1. Set up your account

  1. Sign up on your home country's WU site, e.g. westernunion.com/us, not the Argentine one: you pay in your home currency. Not in the US? Pick your country from Western Union's country list. The website sets your sending country from the site you register on; the app uses the country of your Apple or Google account. You need an email address, a phone number, and a debit card or bank account. There's usually no approval wait.
  2. Start a transfer: enter the amount in dollars, choose Argentina and Cash pick up.
  3. Pay by debit card (see below).
  4. Add yourself as the receiver. Enter your name exactly as it appears on your passport. If your passport has a middle name, include it. The mobile number is optional.
  5. If asked for a transfer reason, choose Repatriation of Funds/Self Transfer.
  6. Keep WU's confirmation email for the transfer. Take four screenshots of it, each zoomed in on one part: Tracking Number (MTCN), Sender Details, Final Receiver Details and Billing Summary. They're the easiest thing to bring to the counter.

2. Keep the cost down

  • Pay by debit card. It's usually the cheapest way that's ready in minutes. A bank account can cost the same but takes up to 4 business days. A credit card or Google Pay costs more, and your bank may add a cash-advance fee. Your bank may ask you to confirm your first transfer.
  • Expect about 7% on $500 from the US outside promotions. New accounts often pay less for a while, and the first transfer may be free.
  • Send fewer, larger transfers. Fees rise more slowly than the amount, so larger transfers usually cost less per dollar. Balance that against how much cash you want to carry, and whether the location has enough pesos.
  • Compare with other options. The rates page shows WU next to Blue and other transfer services.

3. Choose a location

Larger locations

  • In main commercial areas, with many reviews
  • More pesos on hand, so better for larger amounts
  • Usually don't buy US dollar bills

Smaller locations

  • Often short on pesos, so they may not cover larger pickups
  • More likely to buy US dollar bills

Use Find a WU Location to search by address, neighborhood or province and see which locations are open now. Tick "WU branches only" for WU's own offices, which usually have cash. After a visit, tap "Had cash here?" on the location to help others.

4. At the counter

  1. Bring your passport. You can't pick up money without it. Have your address in Argentina handy too, if you didn't enter it when you sent the money (rarely asked).
  2. Wait in line, then hand your passport to the agent and say "Buenas, quiero retirar un envío de dinero." (Hello, I'd like to pick up a money transfer.) Or just hand it over: they'll know why you're there.
  3. When asked for the código, read out your MTCN, or hold your phone up to the window.
  4. The agent may also ask:
    • "¿Cuál es la cantidad?" The amount sent, in its currency (e.g. "USD 500")
    • "¿Cuál es el nombre del remitente?" The sender's name: yours
    • "¿Cuál es su dirección?" Your address in Argentina, as in the Final Receiver Details
  5. A screen beside you then shows three pages. Tap Aceptar on each: Detalles de la transacción (the transfer's details), Protéjase de los fraudes (a fraud warning), and a box where you sign with the pen. This all happens before you get the money.
  6. Watch the agent count the cash in the bill counter. You can ask them to run it through again: "¿Lo puede contar otra vez?"
Not fluent in Spanish? Have the four screenshots of the confirmation email ready (MTCN, Sender Details, Final Receiver Details, Billing Summary) and hold each one up to the glass. Or write the MTCN, amount, sender's name and address on a sheet of paper.

Useful phrases

  • "Buenas, quiero retirar un envío de dinero." (Hello, I'd like to pick up a money transfer.)
  • "¿Qué billetes tienen?" (What bills do you have?) Ask for large bills if you can.
  • "¿Lo puede contar otra vez?" (Can you count it again?)

Timing and safety

  • Go early: lines are shortest at opening, and locations can run out of pesos later in the day.
  • Bring a bag or backpack. A large amount in small bills is a thick stack.
  • Put the cash away before you leave, and consider a taxi afterward for larger amounts.
  • Split large amounts across visits or locations.
  • Note the locations that worked well for next time.
