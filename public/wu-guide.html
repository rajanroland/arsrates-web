How to Get Pesos with Western Union

Last reviewed: October 2026

How it works

You send money from your own US bank account or debit card to yourself, then pick up the pesos in cash at a WU location in Argentina with your passport.

The exchange rate is set when you send. The WU rate on our rates page is WU's rate before fees, so check the rate and fee in the app before you send.

1. Set up your account

  1. Sign up on the US site, westernunion.com/us. The website sets your sending country from the site you register on. The app uses the country of your Apple or Google account.
  2. Link your US bank account or debit card.
  3. Send money to yourself for cash pickup in Argentina. Enter your name exactly as it appears on your passport.
  4. Keep the tracking number (MTCN) WU gives you. You'll need it at the counter.

2. Keep the cost down

  • Compare payment methods. The fee can change with how you pay (bank account or debit card). Check each in the app before you send.
  • Send fewer, larger transfers. Fees rise more slowly than the amount, so larger transfers usually cost less per dollar. Balance that against how much cash you want to carry, and whether the location has enough pesos.
  • Compare with other options. The rates page shows WU next to Blue and other transfer services.

3. Choose a location

Larger locations

  • In main commercial areas, with many reviews
  • More pesos on hand, so better for larger amounts
  • Usually don't buy US dollar bills

Smaller locations

  • Often short on pesos, so they may not cover larger pickups
  • More likely to buy US dollar bills

Use Find a WU Location to search by address, neighborhood or province and see which locations are open now. Tick "WU branches only" for WU's own offices, which usually have cash. After a visit, tap "Had cash here?" on the location to help others.

4. At the counter

Bring

  • Your passport
  • The MTCN, the amount sent, and the sender's name (yours, exactly as entered)
  • Your address in Argentina
Not fluent in Spanish? Write these on a sheet of paper and hand it over with your passport:
  • Sender name (exactly as entered)
  • Passport number
  • MTCN
  • Amount sent, with its currency (e.g. "USD 300")

Useful phrases

  • "Quiero recibir dinero." (I'd like to receive money.)
  • "¿Qué billetes tienen?" (What bills do you have?) Ask for large bills if you can.

Before you leave the counter

  • Check that your details match your passport exactly.
  • Count the cash at the counter.

Timing and safety

  • Go early: lines are shortest at opening, and locations can run out of pesos later in the day.
  • Bring a bag or backpack. A large amount in small bills is a thick stack.
  • Put the cash away before you leave, and consider a taxi afterward for larger amounts.
  • Split large amounts across visits or locations.
  • Note the locations that worked well for next time.
