Western Union vs Changing Cash in Argentina

Last reviewed: November 2026

Short answer: It depends on the day, so check before you send. Western Union gives more pesos when its rate is above Blue by more than your fee. As of November 2026, it's about 7% above Blue, which is unusually wide. At that gap, Western Union ties or wins at every fee we've seen. Much of the time, it hasn't been that far ahead. The fee is added on top of what you send, so a fee of 3% means Western Union has to be more than 3% above Blue to win. This table uses the rates from November 4, 2026: Western Union 1,659 and Blue buy 1,540, a gap of 7.7%.

Which fee will you pay?

Fees vary, so check yours in the app before you compare. These are the amounts we've seen when sending from the US for cash pickup:

  • By debit card: about US$35 on $500 is the most common fee we've seen, and it's the fastest way to pay.
  • Lower debit card fees: some accounts pay less, such as US$15 on $500 and US$65 on $3,000. We don't know how Western Union decides who gets them.
  • New accounts: the first transfer may be free.
  • Paying in person at a Western Union location in the US: about US$8 on $500 and US$23 on $1,500. You can pay with cash or debit, and with a credit card at some locations. Western Union holds the rate for 12 hours, so pay within that time.

How far above Blue is Western Union?

We compared Western Union's daily closing rate with Blue buy on 381 days, from November 2024 to November 2026. We have no direct Western Union readings from early November 2025 to late September 2026.

  • Western Union was above Blue on 64% of those days. Blue was above Western Union on the other 36%, nearly all before October 2025.
  • Western Union was more than 1.6% above Blue on 38% of days, more than 3% above on 22%, and more than 7% above on 3%.
  • Since October 1, 2026, it has been above Blue every day, by at least 4.6%.

So the best choice last year may not be the best choice today. Check the rates page each time you need pesos, and compare Western Union's gap over Blue with your fee. Past fees may also have differed from today's.

Why you might still choose Western Union

  • Your $100 bills are limited. Exchange houses pay the best rate only for bills in perfect condition, and you may want to keep some for things like an apartment deposit.
  • You don't need an Argentine bank account, just your passport.
  • The money is usually ready within minutes, and you can keep drawing on your bank account at home.

Our advice: use Western Union unless Blue is clearly better after the fee, and save your bills for when changing cash really pays. See how to get pesos with Western Union, or find a licensed exchange house.
